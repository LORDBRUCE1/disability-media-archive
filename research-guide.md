# DMA · Forty works, forty research directions

This catalog is for critical research, not a verdict assigning media a "good" or "bad" disability score. Each reading is a **testable thesis** rather than an established consensus. Sources vary between scholarship, filmmaker interviews and reviews; read the original works before publishing definitive scene analyses.

**Geography note:** East and West are filing categories, not cultural essences. *Katawa Shoujo* was made by a transnational team drawing on Japanese visual-novel conventions and is classified **Global**. A society is never a single representational tradition.

## Cross-cultural comparisons worth building exhibits around

### Desire or spectacle? — Katawa Shoujo / 37 Seconds / Margarita with a Straw

The productive question is not whether disability and sexuality may coexist on screen; it is whether the subjects control desire and self-presentation. Compare a romance game structured as player-selected stories with films centering disabled women’s own artistic and romantic decisions. Evaluate authorship, consent, narrative camera and cultural genre, without reproducing mature scenes.

### Exceptional competence as permission — The Good Doctor / Extraordinary Attorney Woo / REAL

These dramas and manga challenge explicit prejudice through excellence, but ask whether the disabled person must be extraordinary to be recognized. The answer may differ by profession, genre and institutional context more than by country.

### Whose language and whose voice? — A Silent Voice / A Sign of Affection / CODA / Hear Me

The central design issue is whose point of view gets to define communication. Track who translates for whom, which language the production privileges, and whether characters who sign or do not speak also get interiority.

### From being displayed to controlling the camera — Freaks / The Elephant Man / I Didn’t See You There / Blind Massage

Investigate the politics of looking. Who is seen, who records, who is credited with interpretation? Consider cinematography, sound and historical institutions instead of assuming camera subjectivity equals authentic access.

### Superpower or accessible medium? — Daredevil / The Vale / Hellblade

A protagonist’s extraordinary sensory experience differs from making a medium usable by disabled audiences. Compare actual interface/access design, formal experimentation and fictional powers.

### Family love and institutional responsibility — Ocean Heaven / With the Light / Speechless / Crip Camp

When narratives focus on caregiving, ask what services are missing from the state and whose labor becomes invisible. Compare family melodrama with disabled-led collective political organization.

### Adaptation is a natural cultural experiment — Forrest Gump / Laal Singh Chaddha

The 2022 Hindi remake of the 1994 American film makes an unusually controlled East–West comparison: the narrative framework is inherited, but the national history, family roles, public institutions and presentation of intimacy change. Instead of declaring one cultural portrayal kinder, ask what each adaptation regards as evidence of independence—and what remains unchanged when an exceptional protagonist succeeds against the odds. See [Yüksel (2026), CINEJ comparative research](https://cinej.pitt.edu/ojs/cinej/article/view/771). *Laal Singh Chaddha* is a comparative companion, not one of the site's forty standalone records.

## East Asia & India

### A Silent Voice (2016) — Japan · Anime / Manga

**Research question:** Can an apology-centered story still deny the harmed person control of the narrative?

**Argument to test:** Yoshitoki Ōima deliberately makes the former bully central and leaves parts of Shōko’s interior life inaccessible. This creates a film about the ethics of recognition and the difficulty of assuming we understand another person. Pay attention to the use of visual isolation, crowd markings and shifting conversational space.

**Read against:** If the strongest perspective belongs to the person seeking forgiveness, disability risks becoming the moral obstacle someone else must overcome. Trace Shōko’s unprompted decisions rather than only the protagonist’s changes.

**Sources:** [Kodansha interview with creator Yoshitoki Ōima](https://kodansha.us/2016/06/08/interview-yoshitoki-oima-on-a-silent-voice/)

### Josee, the Tiger and the Fish (2020) — Japan · Anime / Manga

**Research question:** When does care expand someone’s world, and when is it staged as a rescue romance?

**Argument to test:** The film gives Josee a creative imagination and an ambition independent of her relationship, but also uses her new encounters outside the home as an emotional arc for the nondisabled co-lead. Examine whose point of view governs scenes in public spaces and who takes the decisive actions.

**Read against:** One reading sees interdependence, not dependence: support need not cancel autonomy. The competing reading asks why a romantic protector must initiate so many breakthroughs. BFI offers a pointed critique of the limited access to her perspective.

**Sources:** [BFI Sight & Sound: Josee and perspective](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/josee-the-tiger-and-the-fish-anime-teen-romance)

### A Sign of Affection (2019) — Japan · Anime / Manga

**Research question:** Can learning sign language be a romantic gesture without making the hearing partner its hero?

**Argument to test:** Signing, texting and the layout of manga panels become formal parts of courtship. The creators consulted Deaf specialists, and the series presents Yuki as a university student with interests and friendships beyond communication barriers. Study panel lettering and which character gets internal narration.

**Read against:** The outsider’s process of learning may still be the mechanism through which hearing readers understand Yuki. Ask whether the work also lets her define the norms of the relationship and the language used to represent her.

**Sources:** [Kodansha creator interview: A Sign of Affection](https://kodansha.us/2020/07/21/creator-interview-suu-morishita-talks-a-sign-of-affection-simulpub-debut/)

### REAL (1999) — Japan · Anime / Manga

**Research question:** Does sports manga replace the pitiable disabled body with an equally restrictive heroic masculine ideal?

**Argument to test:** Inoue’s drawings allow wheelchair basketball, anger, rivalry and physical effort to coexist. Analyze bodies in motion and how athletes negotiate masculinity without demanding that every player be inspirational or morally exemplary.

**Read against:** The celebration of elite athleticism can become another test of whose body deserves admiration. Andrea Wood’s scholarship offers a framework for the tension between resisting pity and reinscribing competitive masculinity.

**Sources:** [Andrea Wood, academic article on Inoue’s REAL](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24213739/); [VIZ publisher: REAL manga](https://www.viz.com/real)

### With the Light: Raising an Autistic Child (2000) — Japan · Anime / Manga

**Research question:** Whose voice is a family disability narrative—the autistic child’s, or the caregiver’s?

**Argument to test:** The manga makes community systems, school expectations and kinship pressures visible through a mother’s experiences with her son. This is valuable evidence about how care obligations become public rather than merely private.

**Read against:** Its sympathetic focus on Sachiko can still leave Hikaru’s interior world mediated by others. Compare parental narration with directly autistic-authored perspectives; avoid assuming family testimony is the same as first-person experience.

**Sources:** [Yen Press: With the Light](https://yenpress.com/series/with-the-light?format=Digital); [Yoshiko Okuyama, Reframing Disability in Manga](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvn1tbm8)

### Ranking of Kings (2021) — Japan · Anime / Manga

**Research question:** What happens when a fairy-tale world mistakes communication difference for political incapacity?

**Argument to test:** Bojji is denied the status of a proper ruler because he does not conform to a narrow ideal of strength. The fantasy structure makes judgments about hearing, speech and authority unusually visible; note when his perspective is presented without speech.

**Read against:** The fairy-tale reversal can still equate disability with an extraordinary hidden gift. Ask whether the kingdom ever changes its criteria of kingship, or simply makes an exception for an exceptional hero.

**Sources:** [WIT Studio: Ranking of Kings](https://www.witstudio.co.jp/works/ousama-ranking)

### Violet Evergarden (2018) — Japan · Anime / Manga

**Research question:** Does beautiful animation turn prosthetics into a visual ornament while speaking about trauma?

**Argument to test:** Violet’s hands are narratively tied to postwar survival, mechanical ability, letter-writing and human connection. Compare the detail used to draw her metal hands with the forms of care and labor those hands perform.

**Read against:** One reading treats prostheses as signs of recovered humanity; another asks whether the poetic surface prettifies pain and individualizes the consequences of war. Keep embodied experience separate from metaphorical symbolism.

**Sources:** [Study: disability representation in Violet Evergarden](https://doi.org/10.9734/ajl2c/2026/v9i2323); [Japan Past & Present: selected media about disability in Japan](https://japanpastandpresent.org/en/teaching-aids/reading-lists/media-on-disability-and-japan)

### 37 Seconds (2019) — Japan · Film

**Research question:** What changes when a disabled woman is also the artist and author of her own desires?

**Argument to test:** The central figure is a young manga artist negotiating family authority, paid work and adult identity. Rather than reading her sexuality as scandal or proof of empowerment, connect it to authorship: who gets to draw, speak for and choose the shape of her life?

**Read against:** The casting of an actress with cerebral palsy matters, but authenticity does not make every plot decision beyond critique. Compare the film’s handling of personal agency with stories that treat sexuality as a spectacle for others.

**Sources:** [Los Angeles Times review: 37 Seconds](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/story/2020-01-30/37-seconds-review-hikari)

### The Housekeeper and the Professor (2003) — Japan · Literature

**Research question:** How can a relationship persist when conventional continuity of memory does not?

**Argument to test:** The professor’s short-term memory window shapes the novel’s recurring social rituals, notes and mathematical patterns. Explore the distinction between autobiographical recall and meaningful connection; avoid reducing memory differences to failed personhood.

**Read against:** The housekeeper narrates and orders the story. Does that gently affirm interdependence, or make the professor knowable only through a nondisabled witness? Track repeated scenes and their changing emotional meaning.

**Sources:** [Bibliographic and publication information: The Housekeeper and the Professor](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781250375971/thehousekeeperandtheprofessor/)

### Ocean Heaven (2010) — Mainland China · Film

**Research question:** Is the caregiving parent presented as the real protagonist of disability?

**Argument to test:** The father’s search for support for his adult autistic son exposes the fragile relationship between household responsibility, community institutions and future care. Compare the emotional treatment of paternal sacrifice with practical systems of social support.

**Read against:** The son’s skill and routine are central to the plot, yet the film’s framing can privilege what the father fears and hopes. Ask what the adult son wants and what structural supports would make his life less dependent on family sacrifice.

**Sources:** [Screen Daily: Ocean Heaven production and social subject and synopsis](https://www.screendaily.com/jet-li-to-star-in-first-non-action-drama-ocean-heaven/5004881.article)

### Blind Massage (推拿) (2014) — Mainland China · Film

**Research question:** How can cinema challenge a sighted audience’s assumption that seeing means knowing?

**Argument to test:** Lou Ye’s film makes work, touch, sound, attraction and aesthetic judgment inseparable from a community of blind massage therapists. Chris Berry’s scholarly reading emphasizes that blurred images do not simply simulate vision loss; they also change who holds interpretive authority.

**Read against:** The film’s sensual attention can produce a more embodied account of desire, but can also expose its subjects to voyeuristic fascination. Analyze whose judgments of beauty and intimacy are heard rather than deciding that sensuality is automatically liberating or exploitative.

**Sources:** [Chris Berry, academic chapter: Blind Massage](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/oa-edit/10.4324/9780429507465-13/blind-massage-chris-berry); [Ke Ma: The Politics of Beauty, Sexuality and Disability in Blind Massage](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ke-Ma-30/publication/352197898_The_Politics_of_Beauty_Sexuality_and_Disability_in_Blind_Massage/links/60be8d1e92851cb13d88bf45/The-Politics-of-Beauty-Sexuality-and-Disability-in-Blind-Massage.pdf)

### Still Tomorrow (摇摇晃晃的人间) (2016) — Mainland China · Film

**Research question:** Who owns the story when a disabled poet becomes a national cultural symbol?

**Argument to test:** This documentary follows poet Yu Xiuhua through fame, rural life and complicated personal relationships. Her artistic labor disrupts the easy opposition between disabled dependency and accomplished genius.

**Read against:** An observational documentary can amplify its subject and also create a marketable public persona. Read her poetry alongside the film: how does the voice on the page differ from the woman assembled through editing?

**Sources:** [PBS POV: Still Tomorrow](https://www.pbs.org/pov/films/stilltomorrow/)

### Hear Me (聽說) (2009) — Taiwan · Film

**Research question:** When a movie’s romance runs through sign language, who is assumed to need translation?

**Argument to test:** The film’s sign-language-heavy dialogue foregrounds bodies and gestures rather than speech as the default vehicle of intimacy. Its production alongside the Taipei Deaflympics makes its local cultural setting relevant, not merely decorative.

**Read against:** The film also experiments with the audience’s expectations about who can hear and who cannot. Ask whether its revelation invites viewers to reconsider their assumptions or primarily uses Deafness as a charming romantic device.

**Sources:** [Taipei Times: Hear Me, sign language and the Deaflympics](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2009/08/28/2003452149)

### Extraordinary Attorney Woo (2022) — South Korea · Television

**Research question:** Is professional recognition conditional on being visibly exceptional?

**Argument to test:** The courtroom gives Woo Young-woo a literal speaking role: she argues on behalf of clients and herself. Scholarship highlights the difference between self-advocacy in the plot and earlier stories where disabled people were spoken for.

**Read against:** Her remarkable memory and legal talent may simultaneously reinforce the public belief that an autistic person must be a prodigy to be accepted. Compare how institutions accommodate her to how they would treat an ordinary autistic employee.

**Sources:** [Korean academic article: Extraordinary Attorney Woo and disability](https://kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002900302); [ABA: an autistic lawyer considers Extraordinary Attorney Woo](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/journal/articles/2022/lawyer-with-autism-explores-netflix-series-extraordinary-attorney-woo/)

### Move to Heaven (2021) — South Korea · Television

**Research question:** Does a procedural that deciphers other people’s possessions give an autistic character unusual moral authority?

**Argument to test:** Geu-ru’s meticulous attention allows the drama to turn everyday objects into fragments of lives. His routine is framed as a form of care and interpretive labor, rather than simply an obstacle to social relationships.

**Read against:** The risk is the “autistic detective” formula, where usefulness lies in a gift for patterns. What scenes allow him ordinary uncertainty, humor and frustration without turning them into puzzles to be solved?

**Sources:** [NME: Move to Heaven review](https://www.nme.com/reviews/move-to-heaven-netflix-review-lee-je-hoon-2940498)

### Oasis (오아시스) (2002) — South Korea · Film

**Research question:** Can a romance challenge paternalism without escaping ethical questions about vulnerability and power?

**Argument to test:** Lee Chang-dong’s controversial romance refuses to make disabled desire automatically innocent, inspiring or absent. A feminist disability-media reading connects its family relationships to cultural expectations about care and belonging.

**Read against:** The very attempt to depict unconventional intimacy demands attention to autonomy and consent, not merely the director’s claim to realism. Compare what the film lets the heroine communicate with how others define her needs and choices.

**Sources:** [Kathleen McHugh, Duke University Press, feminist disability reading of Oasis](https://read.dukeupress.edu/camera-obscura/article/40/1%20%28118%29/28/399703/Sexual-and-Intersectional-Region-Family-Disability); [UC Irvine humanities: discussion of Oasis and lived experience](https://www.humanities.uci.edu/events/family-disability-and-lived-experience-lee-chang-dongs-oasis)

### Taare Zameen Par (Like Stars on Earth) (2007) — India · Film

**Research question:** Should a child have to reveal a special talent to deserve educational accommodation?

**Argument to test:** The film dramatizes how disciplinary schooling misreads dyslexia as laziness or disobedience. Its painterly perspective offers a child’s sensory relationship to classrooms, while a teacher creates room for different ways of learning.

**Read against:** The teacher-centered turnaround may imply that one heroic educator can replace systemic change. More pointedly, what if the child were not exceptionally artistic? The right to learn should not depend on being secretly gifted.

**Sources:** [Indian educational research on Taare Zameen Par](https://ejournals.ncert.gov.in/index.php/jie/article/view/3167)

### Margarita with a Straw (2014) — India · Film

**Research question:** Why is a disabled woman’s sexuality treated as an exception rather than ordinary adulthood?

**Argument to test:** Director Shonali Bose links desire to independence, family expectation and geography, informed by conversations with her cousin Malini Chib. The film is useful for exploring how South Asian cinema addresses sexuality beyond the familiar inspirational-biopic frame.

**Read against:** That intention does not settle debates about casting or the camera’s gaze. Compare the subject’s decision-making power, the story’s cultural context, and the limits of representing one woman as an entire disability experience.

**Sources:** [The Guardian: director Shonali Bose on sexuality and disability](https://www.theguardian.com/film/2014/oct/23/margarita-with-a-straw-shonali-bose-cerebral-palsy-india)

### Black (2005) — India · Film

**Research question:** What changes when a transnational adaptation moves Helen Keller’s educational narrative into Indian melodrama?

**Argument to test:** This Hindi-language film reworks the pupil–teacher narrative familiar from The Miracle Worker, emphasizing touch, communication and educational access. Treat the remake itself as evidence: what emotional and visual conventions change across cinematic traditions?

**Read against:** A powerful teacher can become a savior who overshadows the learner’s agency. Compare the girl’s expressive decisions in both films, and ask how gender, family and schooling alter the meaning of independence.

**Sources:** [Research note on the film Black and communication](https://www.worldwidejournals.com/paripex/recent_issues_pdf/2012/September/communication-among-the-physically-challenged-with-special-reference-to-the-film-black_September_2012_4594515595_8503754.pdf); [Described and Captioned Media Program: The Miracle Worker](https://dcmp.org/learn/524-the-miracle-worker)

## Global / transnational

### Katawa Shoujo (2012) — Transnational / online collective · Video Game

**Research question:** When does intimacy make a character more human—and when does an audience turn disability into an object of fascination?

**Argument to test:** Approach the visual novel as a tension between its adult-romance framing and its character-driven writing. The presence of intimacy is not itself evidence of exploitation; consider whether characters have choices, aspirations and disagreements beyond disability, and whether the story allows desire to be mutual rather than merely observed. Its global creators borrow a Japanese genre, so calling it straightforwardly “Eastern representation” would distort its production history.

**Read against:** The game also packages disabled school-age characters within a dating-game structure. Even if it individualizes them, a player may still be encouraged to collect or classify routes through bodily difference. Analyze camera perspective, choice architecture and marketing separately from the writers’ stated intentions; academic commentators disagree over how successfully the work escapes fetishization. Discuss mature content without reproducing it.

**Sources:** [Academic study: digital-game access, representation and sexuality](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/restricted-access/chapter-4.9); [CMU Press: Playing with Feelings, critical chapter](https://press.etc.cmu.edu/file/download/871/1d4abad4-4139-491c-a89b-e7fe15fff0d1)

## Europe & North America

### Daredevil (2015 series) (2015) — United States · Television

**Research question:** What is the difference between a blind hero on screen and a screen accessible to blind viewers?

**Argument to test:** Matt Murdock’s heightened senses invert the “helpless blind person” stereotype; sound design makes sensory perception part of action storytelling. But the series’ initial lack of audio description reveals an industry split between using disability as narrative material and including disabled audiences.

**Read against:** If his powers effectively substitute for sight, has the show escaped the assumption that being a hero requires becoming functionally nondisabled? Distinguish the original 1964 comic character from this specific 2015 adaptation.

**Sources:** [Washington Post: Daredevil launched without audio description](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/act-four/wp/2015/04/14/netflix-makes-a-blind-superhero-accessible-to-blind-viewers/); [Common Sense Media: Daredevil representational critique](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/tv-reviews/daredevil)

### Forrest Gump (1994) — United States · Film

**Research question:** When does supposed innocence make a disabled-coded protagonist a tool for national nostalgia?

**Argument to test:** The film places a man portrayed as cognitively different in major moments of U.S. history. His limited understanding of events helps the viewer experience a moral simplicity that cuts across complicated institutions and politics.

**Read against:** Do not diagnose Forrest with Down syndrome—the film does not establish that. Instead interrogate how the story uses an IQ label, miraculous success and “pure-heartedness” to manufacture authority while limiting the character’s own explanations.

**Sources:** [CINEJ 2026: Forrest Gump and Laal Singh Chaddha compared](https://cinej.pitt.edu/ojs/cinej/article/view/771)

### The Elephant Man (1980) — United Kingdom / United States · Film

**Research question:** When does compassionate spectatorship become another kind of freak show?

**Argument to test:** Lynch first makes the public’s gaze uncomfortable, then stages scientific and high-society encounters that claim to be more civilized. That continuity lets the work question whether pity and clinical curiosity genuinely differ from entertainment.

**Read against:** The film is sympathetic but it can still turn a real historical person into a test of others’ humanity. Compare who gets to speak, interpret the body and determine what a dignified life means.

**Sources:** [BFI Sight and Sound: The Elephant Man](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/elephant-man-how-victorians-dealt-with-unacceptable-inexplicable)

### Freaks (1932) — United States · Film

**Research question:** Does casting disabled performers give them agency when the marketing still sells them as spectacle?

**Argument to test:** Browning employed many performers with visible physical differences and let a community resist outsiders’ cruelty. The work sits at an uneasy historical junction between commercial exploitation and collective solidarity.

**Read against:** The same film relies on horror imagery and an ableist title, so casting authenticity alone is not an ethical verdict. The BBFC’s historical record captures both the initial controversy and later reinterpretations.

**Sources:** [BBFC case study: Freaks, historical controversy and reappraisal](https://www.bbfc.co.uk/case-studies/freaks)

### The Miracle Worker (1962) — United States · Film

**Research question:** Who gets credit when the story of learning language is framed as a teacher’s miracle?

**Argument to test:** Anne Sullivan’s teaching methods offer a potent story about language access as relational labor. The dramatic structure makes communication, patience and the material environment central to education.

**Read against:** By centering a “miracle worker,” the film risks confusing the teacher’s heroism with Helen Keller’s own intellectual achievement. Pair with Black to ask what adaptations preserve or modify that hierarchy.

**Sources:** [Described and Captioned Media Program: The Miracle Worker](https://dcmp.org/learn/524-the-miracle-worker)

### My Left Foot (1989) — Ireland / United Kingdom · Film

**Research question:** Can the virtuoso performance of a nondisabled actor obscure the artist whose life it celebrates?

**Argument to test:** Christy Brown’s writing and painting shift the gaze from an impaired body to authored work, but the film also became emblematic of awards culture rewarding nondisabled performances of disability.

**Read against:** Authenticity is not reducible to an actor’s medical history, but access to roles and professional authority matters. BFI explicitly situates the film within disputes over nondisabled casting.

**Sources:** [BFI: My Left Foot and casting debate](https://player.bfi.org.uk/rentals/film/watch-my-left-foot-1989-online)

### The Diving Bell and the Butterfly (2007) — France / United States · Film

**Research question:** Can cinematography communicate subjectivity without claiming to reproduce another person’s body?

**Argument to test:** The film uses restricted camera positions, letters and imagination to preserve a writer’s voice even when ordinary speech is impossible. Its approach makes cinema itself a question about mediation and the difference between embodiment and authorship.

**Read against:** First-person camera can encourage empathy, yet viewers may mistake stylistic approximation for access to real lived experience. Compare the film’s invented images with Bauby’s first-person memoir.

**Sources:** [Roger Ebert: The Diving Bell and the Butterfly](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-diving-bell-and-the-butterfly-2007)

### Sound of Metal (2019) — United States · Film

**Research question:** Is medical intervention always a restoration, or can it also endanger a new cultural belonging?

**Argument to test:** Subjective sound places the audience close to Ruben’s changing hearing, while Deaf community life challenges the assumption that quiet is a defect. Dialogue around cochlear implants invites discussion of distinct cultural and medical understandings.

**Read against:** The film risks making one Deaf mentor’s position stand for an entire community. Different Deaf and hard-of-hearing people make different choices; debate whose specific experience the film centers.

**Sources:** [LA Times: Deaf performer discusses Sound of Metal](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/awards/story/2021-02-23/paul-raci-sound-of-metal-darius-marder)

### CODA (2021) — United States · Film

**Research question:** Can authentic Deaf casting coexist with a hearing-centered emotional climax?

**Argument to test:** Deaf actors and ASL-informed production give the fishing family specificity, humor and work identities. Yet much of the audience’s emotional journey belongs to Ruby, the hearing daughter who interprets for relatives.

**Read against:** Ask how the film distributes linguistic labor and family responsibility—and whether it portrays Deaf characters as complex people when Ruby is out of the room. The filmmakers themselves emphasize specificity over speaking for all Deaf families.

**Sources:** [Apple TV+: filmmaker and Deaf actors on CODA](https://www.apple.com/tv-pr/news/2021/08/coda-embodies-authentic-representation-onscreen/)

### Crip Camp (2020) — United States · Film

**Research question:** What is lost when disability politics is retold as a triumphant march toward legislation?

**Argument to test:** Co-directed by disabled filmmaker James LeBrecht, the documentary traces friendships and the collective organizing that helped build U.S. disability-rights movements. It replaces individualized triumph with shared political labor.

**Read against:** Victory narratives can flatten ongoing inequality and underrepresent differences within the movement. The film’s impact campaign explicitly sought additional intersectional voices: study its afterlife as advocacy, not just its story arc.

**Sources:** [Crip Camp filmmakers and impact campaign](https://cripcamp.com/); [Journal of American History: Crip Camp review](https://academic.oup.com/jah/article-abstract/108/3/667/6484779)

### Murderball (2005) — United States · Film

**Research question:** Does replacing pity with aggression redefine disability, or simply celebrate conventional masculinity?

**Argument to test:** The athletes are depicted as competitive, funny, flawed and physically ambitious. That roughness refuses the expectation that disabled subjects must be grateful or uplifting.

**Read against:** Its focus on tough men also narrows who gets to embody independent disability. Compare the film’s language of athletic domination with REAL to see where sport expands and limits possible identities.

**Sources:** [Roger Ebert: Murderball review](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/murderball-2005)

### I Didn’t See You There (2022) — United States · Film

**Research question:** What happens when a disabled filmmaker turns the camera away from himself?

**Argument to test:** Reid Davenport’s wheelchair-mounted and handheld footage rejects the demand that disabled people always be visually displayed for others’ comprehension. The technique transforms access, streets and public attention into a question about cinematic point of view.

**Read against:** The film is not a universal portrait of disability; its refusal of the conventional talking-head format is itself a subjective aesthetic choice. Compare the gaze it rejects with Freaks and The Elephant Man.

**Sources:** [PBS POV: I Didn’t See You There](https://www.pbs.org/pov/films/didntseeyouthere/)

### The Peanut Butter Falcon (2019) — United States · Film

**Research question:** When is representation transformed by writing a part around a disabled performer?

**Argument to test:** Zack Gottsagen performs a fictional protagonist with dreams, stubbornness and humor; filmmakers developed the project through their collaboration with him. This provides a concrete alternative to treating disability as merely an acting challenge for nondisabled stars.

**Read against:** Road movies can still simplify support needs into a feel-good lesson for companions. Ask whether the character changes the plot or mainly gives the other men purpose.

**Sources:** [Roger Ebert: The Peanut Butter Falcon and casting](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-peanut-butter-falcon-2019)

### The Intouchables (2011) — France · Film

**Research question:** What happens when disability, race and class are brought together as a friendship comedy?

**Argument to test:** Caregiving is presented through irreverence rather than sanctity, giving two men contrasting experiences of money, work and bodily dependence. Their humor makes interpersonal difference a source of mutual change.

**Read against:** Roger Ebert’s criticism argues the film depends on reductive racial patterns. Examine whether it critiques systems of inequality or uses each man as a remedy for the other’s stereotyped deficiencies.

**Sources:** [Roger Ebert: The Intouchables and racial formula](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-intouchables-2012)

### The Good Doctor (US) (2017) — United States · Television

**Research question:** Does the autistic hero have to be a genius to be permitted in an elite workplace?

**Argument to test:** The U.S. medical drama, adapted from a Korean series, makes institutional prejudice part of its weekly plot. Shaun’s extraordinary diagnostic skill repeatedly earns him credibility with skeptical colleagues.

**Read against:** Autistic commentators disagree: some value the professional visibility; others criticize narrow mannerisms and a lack of autistic creative control. Compare whose clinical needs the hospital meets with the conditional welcome it extends to Shaun.

**Sources:** [TIME: an autistic critic evaluates The Good Doctor](https://time.com/6981442/the-good-doctor-finale-autistic-representation/); [BMJ Medical Humanities: competing view on The Good Doctor](https://stg-blogs.bmj.com/medical-humanities/2017/11/28/abcs-good-doctor-gets-right/)

### Speechless (2016) — United States · Television

**Research question:** Can a family sitcom let a nonspeaking teenager be sharp, difficult and funny without turning him into a lesson?

**Argument to test:** JJ is played by Micah Fowler, an actor with cerebral palsy. The family comedy uses assistive communication and daily conflicts as normal sitcom machinery, not just public-service instruction.

**Read against:** The assertive mother can dominate the story’s voice, repeating the caregiver-centered format the show aims to transcend. Track when JJ shapes a joke or a decision and when others interpret him.

**Sources:** [Television Academy: Speechless and disabled casting](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/press-releases/cast-abcs-speechless-talk-redefining-rules-and-facing-disability-humor-april)

### Sex Education (Isaac storyline) (2019) — United Kingdom · Television

**Research question:** Can a disabled romantic character be flawed without his flaws being blamed on disability?

**Argument to test:** Isaac is played by George Robinson, a wheelchair user. The series makes him witty, protective and sometimes manipulative within a larger ensemble; this resists the expectation that disabled characters must always be model citizens.

**Read against:** Authentic casting does not erase the question of how romantic conflict intersects with stereotypes of jealousy, dependence or desirability. Focus on authorship and audience judgment rather than treating intimate content as proof of progress.

**Sources:** [British Vogue interview with actor George Robinson](https://www.vogue.co.uk/miss-vogue/article/sex-education-george-robinson-isaac)

### Hellblade: Senua’s Sacrifice (2017) — United Kingdom · Video Game

**Research question:** Can interactive design communicate distress without reducing psychosis to a supernatural mechanic?

**Argument to test:** Spatial audio, perception puzzles and consultation with people with lived experience and mental-health researchers make the game a case study in collaborative authorship. Its design frames differences in perception as part of play, not merely backstory.

**Read against:** An evocative audiovisual approximation is not the same as everyone’s lived experience. Critique whether the game’s fantasy conventions clarify or blur the boundary between mental-health experience and horror aesthetics.

**Sources:** [Peer-reviewed JMIR study on Hellblade and psychosis](https://mental.jmir.org/2019/4/e12432/)

### The Vale: Shadow of the Crown (2021) — Canada · Video Game

**Research question:** What if a game designed for blind players also reorganized how sighted players experience play?

**Argument to test:** Its audio-led interface and collaboration with blind and low-vision testers approach access as a design principle. The player’s blindness is not just a character trait: it alters the form of the game itself.

**Read against:** Accessible mechanics do not automatically settle the representation of a fictional blind hero. Compare its sound-centered choices with visual substitutes for sight used by supernatural heroes like Daredevil.

**Sources:** [Falling Squirrel developer: The Vale and accessibility](https://www.fallingsquirrel.com/the-vale); [Falling Squirrel: launch and CNIB collaboration](https://www.fallingsquirrel.com/post/launch-day)

### Flowers for Algernon (1966) — United States · Literature

**Research question:** When does “becoming intelligent” expose the cruelty of society rather than the deficiency of the person?

**Argument to test:** Charlie’s written progress reports make literacy, social standing and the narrator’s changing self-understanding inseparable. The book offers a formal way to ask who sets standards for intelligence and human worth.

**Read against:** The language of enhancement can still accept a hierarchy in which greater cognitive capacity earns humanity. Distinguish the 1959 short story from the expanded 1966 novel; the year 1969 in the original PDF is not the novel’s first publication date.

**Sources:** [WorldCat: 1966 Flowers for Algernon novel](https://search.worldcat.org/title/232370); [SparkNotes: 1959 story and 1966 novel history](https://www.sparknotes.com/lit/algernon/facts/)
